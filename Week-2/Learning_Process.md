# Listening and Active Communication 

**Q1. What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**
    Answer: The steps to do Active Listening are:- 

    1.Give the speaker your full attention. 
    2.Avoid distractions and internal thoughts. 
    3.Let, the person finish speaking. 
    4.Don't Interrupt in between. 
    5.Use "door openers" to encourage the speaker continue. 
    6.Use appropriate body language to show that you are listening. 
    7.Take notes, wherever needed. 
    8.Paraphrase what the person has said to confirm that we have understand. 

**Q2. According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?**

According to Fisher's model, reflective listening means listening carefully and showing the speaker that you understand the message. 

The key points are :- 
    1.Listen carefully.
    2.Reflect the message.
    3.Reflect emotions.
    4.Observe the body language.
    5.Verify your understanding.
    6.Avoid assumptions.
    7.Clarify when needed. 

**Q3.What are the obstacles in your listening process?**
Answer: Some obstacles in my listening process are - 

    1.Distractions
    2.Thinking about my response. 
    3.Interrupting
    4.Making assumptions
    5.Lack of concentration
**Q4.What can you do to improve your listening?**
Answer: I can improve my listening by:

    1.Giving the speaker my full attention.
    2.Avoiding distractions such as checking my phone while someone is talking.
    3.Allowing the person to finish speaking before I respond.
    4.Asking questions when I do not understand something.
    5.Taking notes during important meetings or technical discussions.
    6.Paraphrasing the speaker's message to check whether I understood it correctly.
    7.Paying attention to body language, facial expressions, and emotions.
    8.Avoiding assumptions and listening to the complete message before forming an opinion.
    9.Giving small responses such as "I understand", "I see", or "Tell me more" to show that I am listening.

**Q6 When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?**
Answer: I switch to aggressive communication when I become very frustrated or angry, especially when I feel that someone is not listening to me.
I am expressing what I want, but I am doing it in a disrespectful or forceful way.
Instead of solving the problem, aggressive communication can make the other person defensive and can turn a small disagreement into a bigger conflict.

**Q8. How can you make your communication assertive? You can watch and analyse the videos, then think what would be a few steps you can apply in your own life?**
Answer: After watching the videos, I understand that being assertive does not mean being rude, aggressive, or trying to control other people. It means standing up for myself and clearly expressing my thoughts, feelings, needs, and opinions while also respecting the other person.

I can improve my communication and become more assertive by following these steps:

**Stop avoiding difficult conversations**

Instead of staying silent just to avoid conflict, I should communicate my problem calmly. If I keep suppressing my feelings, frustration can build up and may later come out as anger or aggression.

**Say what I really mean**

I should avoid saying "yes" when I actually mean "no" just because I am afraid of disappointing someone. I can politely say no and explain my reason when necessary.

**Be direct and clear**

I should not expect people to understand my feelings or needs without me explaining them. I should clearly say what I need instead of using hints, sarcasm, or indirect comments.

**Use "I" statements**

Instead of blaming the other person, I can explain how a situation affects me.

For example:

Aggressive:
"You never listen to me."

Assertive:
"I feel ignored when I am interrupted. I would like to finish what I am saying first."

**Control my tone and body language**

Being assertive is not only about the words I use. I should maintain a calm and confident tone, appropriate eye contact, and confident body language.

**Listen to the other person**

Assertiveness is not about only making my point. I should also listen to the other person's opinion and allow them to speak without interrupting.

**Respect both sides**

I should stand up for my own rights without putting down the other person's needs or opinions. If there is a disagreement, I should look for a compromise instead of trying to force my decision.

**Deal with problems early**

If something is bothering me, I should communicate it before it becomes a bigger problem. This can prevent the situation from moving from passive communication to frustration and eventually aggressive communication.

**Avoid passive-aggressive behaviour**

Instead of using sarcasm, taunts, gossip, or the silent treatment, I should directly explain what is bothering me.

For example:

Passive-aggressive:
"Fine, do whatever you want."

Assertive:
"I don't agree with this decision. Can we discuss it before we decide?"
